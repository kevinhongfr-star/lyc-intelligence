# Comms Methodology — Agency Pitch Framework
*Source: Sheet 9 — LYC Unified Strategy Tracker. Adapted from NEXUS GTM process for LYC brand rollout.*

---

## Core Principles (5)

| # | Principle | Definition |
|---|-----------|------------|
| 1 | **Taxonomy Principle** | Outcomes lead. Methods follow. Taxonomy recedes. Users should know what they're getting and why before they learn internal framework names. |
| 2 | **Decision Gates, Not Revisions** | Each phase ends with a lock. Once locked, it doesn't reopen unless new information changes the underlying assumption. Prevents scope drift. |
| 3 | **Anti-Positioning First** | You don't win by saying you're better. You win by changing the frame of the comparison. Define who you're positioning against and how you reframe. |
| 4 | **One Engine, Many Voices** | Every core idea propagates across voices and channels. Strategy defines who says what, where, and to what end. |
| 5 | **The Product Never Speaks First** | Thought leader builds the category. Product voice explains the solution. Institutional voice closes enterprise sale. Nobody feels sold to. |

---

## Phase Overview (6 Phases)

| Phase | Title | What Gets Decided | Duration |
|-------|-------|-------------------|----------|
| 1 | Brand Strategy & Positioning | Category, brand architecture, voice, tagline, visual system | 1-2 days |
| 2 | Messaging Architecture & Content Strategy | Message layers, audience messaging, anti-positioning, content pillars, content engine, objections | 2-3 days |
| 3 | Launch Strategy & Campaign Architecture | Launch timeline, campaign phases, content calendar, asset inventory | 1-2 days |
| 4 | Partnerships, Ecosystem & KPI Framework | Partner strategy, ecosystem integration, pricing tiers, KPIs, measurement plan | 1-2 days |
| 5 | Cross-Business Handoff (Optional) | How product feeds other business units (B2C→B2B, product→services) | 1 day |
| 6 | Unified Flywheel Integration (Optional) | How product plugs into broader business flywheel — asset mapping, calendar, new North Star | 1 day |

## Phase 1 — Brand Strategy & Positioning
**Goal:** What is this thing, and why should anyone care enough to remember it?

| Decision | What It Answers |
|----------|-----------------|
| Category | What do people compare us to? e.g., Executive Intelligence Platform (not AI tool) |
| Brand Architecture | How does this relate to the parent brand? Endorsed, sub-brand, standalone, house of brands |
| Tagline / Strap Line | The 8-word version of the promise. e.g., "Know where you stand. Know where to go." |
| Voice | How do we sound? e.g., Sharp, calm, peer-to-peer, no hype |
| Visual System | What do we look like? Colors, typography, design rules, motion principles |
| Taxonomy Rule | How do we talk about internal frameworks? Outcomes lead. Methods follow. Taxonomy recedes. |

**Decision Gate:** Lock all six brand decisions before moving to Phase 2. If the category is wrong, everything downstream is wrong.

---

## Phase 2 — Messaging Architecture & Content Strategy
**Goal:** What do we say, to whom, through whom, and why does it work?

| Deliverable | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| 1. 3-Layer Message Architecture | Brand message (why) → Product message (what) → Feature message (how). Brand opens. Product explains. Features close. |
| 2. Audience-Specific Messaging | For each segment: who they are, their quiet question, how we talk to them, what they buy first, ecosystem exit path. |
| 3. Anti-Positioning Map | For each competitor: the frame they use, how we reframe it, the quiet insult (implied contrast that stings but is true). |
| 4. Content Pillars | 3-5 buckets organizing all content. Each with: what it is, why it works, examples, cadence, funnel job. |
| 5. Multi-Voice, Multi-Channel Engine | Voice table, channel map, repurposing engine diagram (one input → many outputs), conversion path across voices. |
| 6. Objection Handling | Top 5-6 objections, each with 3-part response: validate → reframe → land value. |

**Decision Gate:** Lock audience segments, content pillars, and voice architecture before moving to Phase 3.

---

## Phase 3 — Launch Strategy & Campaign Architecture
**Goal:** How do we actually launch this — in what sequence, with what assets, over what timeframe?

| Deliverable | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| 1. Launch Timeline | Week-by-week breakdown. Standard: W1 Build&Seed → W2 Problem Phase → W3 Launch → W4 Proof&Momentum. |
| 2. Campaign Phases | For each phase: strategic goal, key messages, content plan by voice/channel, asset list, success metric. |
| 3. Content Calendar | Week-by-week across all voices and channels, mapped to campaign phases. |
| 4. Asset Inventory | Everything to build: landing pages, email sequences, video scripts, social content, design assets, workshop decks. |
| 5. Launch Offer Structure | Pricing tiers, founding member offer, referral program timing, partner launch timing. |

**Decision Gate:** Lock timeline, campaign structure, and launch offer before Phase 4.

---

## Phase 4 — Partnerships, Ecosystem & KPI Framework
**Goal:** How does this connect to everything else, and how do we know it's working?

| Deliverable | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| 1. Partnership Strategy | Tier 1/2/3 partners, affiliate/referral structure, co-marketing opportunities, activation timing. |
| 2. Ecosystem Integration Map | What feeds this product, what this product feeds, shared assets, data compounding effects. |
| 3. Pricing Architecture | Tiered model, volume/enterprise pricing, discount logic, free trial/freemium strategy, price positioning. |
| 4. KPI Framework | Awareness → Activation → Retention → Revenue → Referral → Ecosystem. Metrics for each stage. |
| 5. North Star Metric | The one number that matters most. If it's going up, everything else is probably working too. |

**Decision Gate:** Lock KPI framework and North Star metric. Partnerships and pricing can be adjusted post-launch.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid (6)

| Pitfall | Why It's a Problem |
|---------|-------------------|
| Skipping Phase 1 | Everyone wants to talk about launch tactics. Positioning is 80% of the work. Get it right first. |
| Doing everything in one meeting | The brain can only absorb so much. One phase at a time. One decision at a time. |
| Revisiting locked decisions | "I just had another idea" is not new information. New competitor or market data? That's a reason. |
| Leading with taxonomy | Resist showing off frameworks and clever names. Audience doesn't care what you call it. They care what it does. |
| Forgetting anti-positioning | Every category has incumbents. If you don't define how you're different, people default to price and features. |
| No North Star metric | If you don't know what winning looks like, you'll never know if you won. Pick one thing. Measure it obsessively. |
