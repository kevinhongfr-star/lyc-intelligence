## 1. BRAND PROMISE & POSITIONING

### 1.1 Brand Promise

**LYC — Leadership Intelligence**

Clean. Category-defining. No modifiers. Global scope with Asia depth as the proof point, not the boundary.

### 1.2 Positioning Statement

LYC is a leadership intelligence firm. We diagnose gaps, design better teams, and deliver executive impact — across self-serve platforms, live programmes, and retained search.

### 1.3 Core Differentiator

LYC is the only leadership intelligence firm that combines:
- A self-serve diagnostic platform (NEXUS)
- Live programmes and coaching (workshops, webinars, cohorts)
- Retained executive search and advisory

All built on the same IP stack. Three layers of engagement. One system.

### 1.4 The Gap We Close

Most organizations make leadership decisions based on assumptions from a different era. LYC gives you the intelligence to make them based on what comes next.

### 1.5 What We Are Not

- We are not an executive search firm that also does advisory
- We are not a consulting firm that also does recruiting
- We are not a SaaS platform
- We are a leadership intelligence firm — intelligence is the category, search and advisory are how we deliver it at the highest level

### 1.6 Anti-Positioning

**Primary antagonist: Traditional executive search firms**

Their frame: "We have the best network and the most rigorous process."

Our reframe: "Traditional search firms evaluate past performance. We evaluate future capability. The candidates who succeeded in the last role aren't always the ones who'll succeed in the next one — especially when the environment is changing this fast. We start with intelligence about what the role actually requires, then find the people who fit that future."

**Secondary antagonists (situational):**
- vs. Leadership advisory firms: "Advisory firms give you recommendations. Search firms give you candidates. We give you both — connected end to end."
- vs. HR assessment companies: "Standard assessments measure personality traits. Leadership intelligence measures leadership capability in context."
- vs. AI coaching tools: "AI tools are great for skills. Leadership decisions require judgment. NEXUS is our intelligence platform — it gives you the data. But when the decision matters, you want human judgment that comes from having placed hundreds of executives across APAC."

## 2. THE UNIFIED FLYWHEEL (SERVICE ARCHITECTURE)

Three layers. Two bridging mechanisms. One reverse loop. This is the business.

### 2.1 Layer 1 — Leadership Intelligence

The entry point. The data layer. The proof.

**What it is:**
- NEXUS platform (self-serve diagnostics, assessments, benchmarks)
- Thought leadership (webinars, newsletter, briefs, podcast, YouTube)
- Kevin's voice + all partner voices across all channels

**Customer question it answers:** "Where do I stand?"

**What powers it:** NEXUS, DEX, SHIFT diagnostics, LENS data

**Purpose:** attract, educate, baseline, prove

### 2.2 Layer 2 — Leadership Programmes

The middle layer. The skill-building. The revenue bridge.

**What it is:**
- 27 workshop modules (3 content pillars)
- 1:1 coaching + cohort programmes
- Council + peer forums
- Webinars (top of Layer 2, feeds Layer 1 too)

**Customer question it answers:** "How do I get better?"

**What powers it:** 27 workshop modules, 5 advisory lanes, Council framework

**Purpose:** develop capability, generate mid-tier revenue, qualify for Layer 3

### 2.3 Layer 3 — Executive Search & Leadership Advisory

The core. The retained business. The highest margin.

**What it is:**
- Retained executive search (C-suite + board)
- Leadership advisory (organizational design, team effectiveness, succession)
- Board advisory + governance

**Customer question it answers:** "Who do I need, and how do we win?"

**What powers it:** search practice, TRIDENT, MOSAIC, PRISM

**Purpose:** highest-value client relationships, category authority, premium revenue

### 2.4 Bridging Mechanism A — The Maturity Stack

Content → community → conversation. 4 stages, 90 days. No one skips a stage with a sales pitch.

(See Section 7 for full detail)

### 2.5 Bridging Mechanism B — The Graduation Path

Programme participants who need more move to retained services. It's not upsell — it's deepening.

- Workshop → advisory engagement (team-level issues surfaced)
- Coaching → search (individual ready for next move)
- Council → board advisory / retained work (executive team needs ongoing support)

### 2.6 The Reverse Loop (Layer 3 → Layer 1)

This is what makes it a flywheel, not a funnel.

- Search candidates → NEXUS assessments (data + user growth)
- Advisory clients → team diagnostics (NEXUS usage + data)
- Programme participants → NEXUS ongoing access (subscriptions + retention)
- All LYC clients → content + community (thought leadership fuel + case studies)

### 2.7 The Ambient Brand Principle

Every layer of the flywheel is visible from every other layer — just not front and center. Someone reading a newsletter knows LYC does search and advisory. Someone using NEXUS knows there's a team behind it. Someone in a workshop knows there are deeper advisory engagements available.

They're not being sold to. They just know it's there. When they're ready, they know where to find you.

### 2.8 What Makes It Spin — Three Forces

**Force 1 — Content Engine (3 pillars, continuous)**
- 3 webinars/month + 4 workshops/month = 7 live events
- Newsletter 2x/month + podcast 1x/week + daily social content
- One core insight → 7 repurposes

**Force 2 — NEXUS Platform (data compounding)**
- More users → more data → better benchmarks → better product → more users
- More assessments → better candidate signals → better placements → more referrals

**Force 3 — Client Success Stories (social proof compounding)**
- Every placement → case study → content → new prospects
- Every engagement → benchmark data → thought leadership → new leads
- Every graduate → NEXUS user + community member + potential candidate/client

### 2.9 Revenue Split

- **LYC B2B (Layers 2 + 3): 85-90% of total revenue**
- **NEXUS B2C2B (Layer 1 + handoff revenue): 10-15% of total revenue**

NEXUS is always embedded in LYC communication but never overshadows the B2B positioning. NEXUS is the gateway to the ecosystem, not the main event.

## 3. THE TAXONOMY RULE

One rule, applied everywhere.

**Outcomes lead. Methods follow. Taxonomy recedes.**

### 3.1 The Three Levels

**Level 1 — Primary (customer-facing, large/visible)**
Outcome language only. No framework names. No code names.
- Example: "Leadership Diagnostic" (not "SHIFT Assessment")
- Example: "China Leadership Briefing" (not "NEXUS Report")
- Example: "Executive Team Assessment" (not "MOSAIC Diagnostic")

**Level 2 — Secondary (customer-facing, supporting text)**
Type and category names. Still no framework names unless they're product brands.
- Example: "Executive team assessment · 15 minutes · benchmarked against 200+ China leaders"

**Level 3 — Tertiary (small text, footnotes, "powered by")**
Framework/IP names live here. Background information.
- Example: "Powered by SHIFT-LEAP"
- Example: "Methodology: PRISM framework"

### 3.2 The NEXUS Exception

NEXUS is a product brand with its own identity and URL. It gets Level 1 billing on its own surfaces. On LYC surfaces, it's still Level 1 when described as a platform, but the individual assessments inside NEXUS follow the rule.

On LYC surfaces, NEXUS is described as "our leadership intelligence platform" — method, not product.

### 3.3 Where This Applies

- Website (every page)
- All decks and proposals
- LinkedIn profiles and posts
- Email outreach
- Workshop descriptions
- Webinar landing pages
- Newsletter headers
- All client-facing templates
- Everything a client or prospect sees

## 4. MESSAGING ARCHITECTURE

Every communication operates at three levels simultaneously. Know which level leads.

### 4.1 Level 1 — Brand Message (Why)

The category claim. The reason someone stops scrolling.

> "Most organizations make leadership decisions based on assumptions from a different era. LYC gives you the intelligence to make them based on what comes next."

This is the opening move. It doesn't mention search. It doesn't mention advisory. It mentions the problem and the category of solution.

### 4.2 Level 2 — Service Message (What)

What you actually offer, framed as three entry points to one system. Not a list — a system.

> "LYC delivers leadership intelligence across three layers of engagement: self-serve diagnostics and assessments, live programmes and coaching, and retained executive search and advisory. Same IP, three ways to engage."

This is where you explain the flywheel in plain language. The customer doesn't need to know it's called a flywheel. They need to intuit that you're not just another search firm.

### 4.3 Level 3 — Feature / Proof Message (How)

The specific things you do, always tied to an outcome.

- Benchmark your leadership team against 200+ China-based executives
- Executive assessment in 15 minutes — 7 dimensions, instant report
- Workshops on AI leadership, China market entry, executive team alignment
- Retained search for C-suite and board-level roles across APAC
- Advisory engagements on organizational design and leadership capability

### 4.4 The Rule

Brand message opens. Service message explains the system. Feature message delivers proof.

You never lead with a framework name. You never lead with "search." You lead with the problem.

### 4.5 Audience-Specific Messaging

**CEO — Enters at Layer 2 or 3**
Quiet question: "Do we have the right leadership team for what's coming?"
Entry message: "Most leadership teams were built for the strategy you had three years ago. Is yours built for the one you need now?"

**CHRO — Enters at Layer 1 or 2**
Quiet question: "How do I build a leadership pipeline for the future?"
Entry message: "Succession planning usually relies on performance reviews from people who work for you. What if you had an objective baseline?"

**PE / Investors — Enters at Layer 1 or 3**
Quiet question: "Can this leadership team execute the investment thesis?"
Entry message: "Due diligence covers financials, markets, and technology. Leadership quality is usually a guess. It shouldn't be."

## 5. NEXUS HANDOFF FRAMEWORK (ENDORSED BRAND MODEL)

### 5.1 The Principle

NEXUS is a method, not a product, when it appears on LYC surfaces.

- On NEXUS's own site: it's a product with its own identity. Correct.
- On LYC's site, in LYC's decks, from LYC's partners' mouths: NEXUS is "our leadership intelligence platform" or "our diagnostic engine." It's the tool we use to deliver insight. It's not the thing we sell.

### 5.2 What This Sounds Like

**Wrong (product-first):**
"Try NEXUS, our executive intelligence platform. Starting at $25/month."

**Right (B2B-first, platform as method):**
"Every search and advisory engagement begins with a diagnostic — powered by our NEXUS intelligence platform — so we're working from data, not assumptions."

**Wrong (SaaS framing):**
"Subscribe to NEXUS for ongoing leadership development."

**Right (B2B framing):**
"We use NEXUS to baseline leadership capability at the start of an engagement, track progress throughout, and measure outcomes at the end."

### 5.3 The Handoff Message

When a NEXUS user is ready for more, the message isn't "upgrade your subscription." It's:

> "The data suggests you'd benefit from working with our advisory team directly. Here's what that looks like."

It's a referral, not an upsell. Same brand family. Different level of engagement.

### 5.4 Brand Relationship

- NEXUS = the platform / the tool / self-serve (B2C2B entry point)
- LYC = the firm / the team / the retained service (B2B core)
- "NEXUS by LYC" is always visible on NEXUS — connection established from day one
- Tone shift: NEXUS = direct, conversational, peer-to-peer (colleague voice). LYC = professional, authoritative, consultative (advisor voice).
- Same intelligence, same clarity, same no-hype style. Different register.

## 7. MATURITY STACK — B2C → B2B GRADUATION

Four stages. Users move through them sequentially. You never skip a stage with a sales pitch.

### 7.1 Stage 0 — Self-Serve User

- What it is: Someone using NEXUS on their own
- Relationship: Transactional. They're using a tool.
- Trigger to next stage: They complete their first assessment and see value
- What we offer: Content invitations — not sales

### 7.2 Stage 1 — Insight Consumer

- What it is: They attend a webinar, subscribe to the newsletter, read a briefing
- Relationship: Intellectual. They respect the thinking.
- Trigger to next stage: They consume 2-3 pieces of content and engage (open rates, attendance, Q&A)
- What we offer: Community invitations

**Stage 1 Touchpoints:**
- Webinar invitations (tied to assessment results)
- Newsletter (Leadership Intelligence Brief, 2x/month)
- Board Briefs / white papers
- Podcast episodes (personalized recommendations)
- YouTube tutorials / short-form clips

### 7.3 Stage 2 — Community Member

- What it is: They join a peer roundtable, come to a private briefing, guest on the podcast, participate in a Council-style forum
- Relationship: Belonging. They see themselves in the room.
- Trigger to next stage: They attend 1-2 community events and express interest in going deeper
- What we offer: A conversation with the LYC team

**Stage 2 Touchpoints:**
- Private peer roundtables (8-10 executives, Chatham House rules)
- Executive Briefings (quarterly, invite-only, 90 minutes)
- Podcast guest invites
- Council-style forums (curated peer groups)
- Office hours / live Q&A (monthly)

### 7.4 Stage 3 — LYC Client / Candidate

- What it is: Coaching, workshops, advisory, search, or placement
- Relationship: Commercial + trusted advisor

### 7.5 The Sequencing Rule

First invite = content (Stage 1).
Second invite = community (Stage 2), but only if they engaged with the first.
Third invite = conversation (Stage 3), but only if they engaged with the second.

No one gets a sales email before they've said yes to two invitations that aren't sales.

### 7.6 Four Handoff Paths Through the Stack

**Path 1 — Individual Executive → Coaching / Programmes**
- 0→1: Relevant webinar + newsletter
- 1→2: Peer roundtable or podcast guest spot
- 2→3: "Want to talk about what 1:1 coaching built on your NEXUS data would look like?"
- Target: 5% of Pro tier, 15% of Executive tier

**Path 2 — Team Leader → Team Assessment + Workshop**
- 0→1: Team-leadership webinar + relevant briefing
- 1→2: Peer roundtable for function heads
- 2→3: Team assessment + workshop conversation
- Target: 3-5% of Director+ users

**Path 3 — CHRO / Talent Leader → Organizational Assessment + Advisory**
- Fast-track: Skip Stage 0→1 individual content, go straight to Stage 1.5 (CHRO-specific briefing)
- 1→2: CHRO-only roundtable or executive briefing
- 2→3: Intro to advisory partner
- Target: 8-10% of CHRO/talent leader users

**Path 4 — High-Caliber Candidate → Search Placement**
- 0→1: Premium content (webinars, briefs) — intelligence mode
- 1→2: Roundtable or podcast (validates quality, warms relationship)
- 2→3: Search outreach from recruiter (separate channel, no NEXUS data disclosed)
- Ethics: TOS disclosure + opt-out. Data used internally, never revealed in outreach.
- Target: 20% of placements from NEXUS-identified candidates

### 7.7 Maturity Speed

Target: 90 days from NEXUS sign-up to first LYC commercial conversation, moving through all three stages.

# PART 2 — BRAND CHECKLIST

> **Every agent: run this checklist before publishing any client-facing content.**
> Brand steward spot-checks. If in doubt, ask Emily.

---

## POSITIONING CHECK

- Does it lead with the problem, not the service?
- Is LYC described as a "leadership intelligence" firm (not "executive search firm")?
- Is search described as one of three layers, not the main thing?
- Is the tone: sharp, calm, peer-to-peer, no hype?

## TAXONOMY CHECK

- Do framework/IP names appear only in Level 3 (small text / "powered by")?
- Are assessment names functional (e.g., "Leadership Diagnostic") not codenames (e.g., "SHIFT Assessment")?
- Is NEXUS described as "our leadership intelligence platform" (not a "SaaS product" or "subscription tool")?
- No D³ / three dots / internal scaffolding in external materials (unless specifically approved)?

## SERVICE ARCHITECTURE CHECK

- Are services described as three layers (Intelligence / Programmes / Executive Search & Leadership Advisory)?
- Is it clear they're one system, not a list?
- Does the customer question get answered (Where do I stand? / How do I get better? / Who do I need?)?

## NEXUS HANDOFF CHECK

- Is NEXUS "by LYC" (endorsed brand) visible on NEXUS surfaces?
- Is the handoff framed as "graduation" or "go deeper" — not "upgrade" or "upsell"?
- Is the message "NEXUS gives you the data. LYC helps you act on it."?
- No pricing or subscription talk on LYC surfaces (that stays on NEXUS)?

## MATURITY STACK CHECK

- Are we inviting to content first (Stage 1), not selling?
- Have they engaged with Stage 1 before we invite to Stage 2?
- Have they engaged with Stage 2 before we offer a LYC conversation?
- Exception: CHRO/talent leaders can fast-track to Stage 1.5

## AMBIENT B2B CHECK

- Are LYC B2B services visible (search, advisory, workshops) — present but not pushed?
- Is there a clear "What we do" or "Services" link/section somewhere on the page/email/post?
- Nobody should reach the end of a piece of content and not know LYC does search and advisory — but they also shouldn't feel sold to.

## VOICE CHECK

- Kevin voice: sharp, calm, peer-to-peer, contrarian insight, no fluff
- Institutional voice (company page, templates, decks): authoritative, clear, concise, same intelligence standard
- NEXUS voice: direct, conversational, smart colleague — slightly more casual than LYC but same no-BS style
- No consulting-firm generic language. No AI-tool marketing hype.

### Rollout Principles

1. **No external announcement.** The website relaunch is the signal moment. People notice the change gradually.
2. **Internal alignment > external fanfare.** The team needs to speak the same language before anything goes live.

5. **Kevin spot-checks, doesn't do line edits.** Agents execute. Emily audits. Kevin reviews key decisions.
