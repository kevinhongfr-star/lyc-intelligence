## 1. Model Explanation

A 30-item competitive positioning assessment measuring leadership positioning across five dimensions: Market, Capability, Timing, Risk, and Impact. LEAP classifies leaders into 14 archetypes based on their dominant dimension pairs, with a unique 5-tier dimension verdict system (Strong Primary, Strong Secondary, Reserve, Weak, Out of Scope) that captures both the leader's strengths and their deployable range.

The assessment measures 5 dimensions with 30 items on a 5-point Likert (1 = Strongly Disagree, 5 = Strongly Agree) scale. Each dimension is normalised to a 0–20 score, and the composite is expressed as a 0–100 percentile.

The archetype system classifies leaders into 14 archetypes based on Each archetype is defined by a specific pair of dominant dimensions (the two highest-scoring dimensions). With 5 dimensions, there are 10 possible pairs. Additionally, there are 4 single-dimension or special-context archetypes for a total of 14. The dimension verdict system uses 5 tiers (Strong Primary, Strong Secondary, Reserve, Weak, Out of Scope) to indicate how deployable each dimension is..

## 3. Archetype Detail

### A1 — Catalyst
**Dominant Pair:** Market × Timing  

**Core Pattern:** Drives change through energy and momentum — senses market shifts and acts at the right moment

**Strengths:**
- Excellent market sensing — picks up on competitive shifts early
- Superior timing judgment — knows when to move and when to wait
- Creates momentum that others can follow
- Captures opportunity windows that more cautious leaders miss

**Growth Areas:**
- May move faster than organisational capacity can absorb
- Risk of momentum without depth — moving fast but not building durability
- Capability depth may lag behind market awareness
- May underestimate the execution required to sustain momentum

**Development Focus:** Build capability depth to sustain momentum; develop risk assessment to avoid over-commitment; build organisational change management alongside market sensing.

**Best-Fit Context:** Fast-changing markets; turnarounds requiring rapid momentum; market entry timing; new initiative launches.

### A2 — Market Strategist
**Dominant Pair:** Market × Capability  

**Core Pattern:** Builds systems and capabilities calibrated to competitive reality

**Strengths:**
- Deep market understanding paired with clear capability differentiation
- Builds capabilities that match market positioning needs
- Strong alignment between what the market needs and what they can deliver
- Good at translating market insight into capability development

**Growth Areas:**
- Timing may be less precise — strong on what to build, less precise on when
- May over-design systems for current market conditions
- Impact and influence scope may be narrower than strategic insight
- Risk of strategic analysis without decisive action

**Development Focus:** Develop timing judgment — build instinct for when to act; build impact and influence capability to translate strategy into results; develop risk assessment for strategic moves.

**Best-Fit Context:** Strategic positioning; capability building for competitive advantage; market analysis and planning; long-term competitive positioning.

### A3 — Pioneer
**Dominant Pair:** Timing × Risk  

**Core Pattern:** Moves into new territory before competitors recognise the opening

**Strengths:**
- Exceptional timing — sees windows before they are obvious
- Willingness to take calculated risks that others avoid
- First-mover advantage in new markets or new contexts
- Comfort with uncertainty and incomplete information

**Growth Areas:**
- May underestimate the capability required to deliver on first-mover position
- Market depth may be thinner — strong on timing, weaker on competitive analysis depth
- Risk of moving too far ahead of market readiness
- Impact and influence may not scale with the risk taken

**Development Focus:** Deepen market analysis to validate timing judgments; build capability depth to capitalise on first-mover position; develop impact scaling to ensure the move delivers measurable results.

**Best-Fit Context:** Market entry; new initiative launch; first-mover opportunities; high-uncertainty environments where speed matters.

### A4 — Founder
**Dominant Pair:** Capability × Impact  

**Core Pattern:** Creates something durable from a position of differentiated strength

**Strengths:**
- Strong differentiated capability — clear value proposition
- Ability to translate capability into measurable impact
- Builds durable structures and lasting value
- Strong at demonstrating individual contribution to outcomes

**Growth Areas:**
- May be slower to recognise market shifts — strong on building, weaker on scanning
- Timing may not be a strength — builds well, but not necessarily at the optimal moment
- Risk orientation may be more conservative — prefers known paths
- May build capability that outpaces market demand

**Development Focus:** Develop market sensing to ensure capability is aligned with market needs; build timing judgment for when to build and when to pause; develop risk comfort for uncertain environments.

**Best-Fit Context:** Capability building; organisational founding or re-founding; establishing new functions; creating durable competitive advantage through capability.

### A5 — Anchor
**Dominant Pair:** Risk × Capability  

**Core Pattern:** Creates stability in turbulent market conditions through deep capability and risk discipline

**Strengths:**
- Deep capability provides stable foundation
- Strong risk assessment and calibration
- Creates stability in volatile or uncertain contexts
- Reliable and consistent — dependable in crisis

**Growth Areas:**
- May be too conservative — risk awareness can lead to excessive caution
- Market orientation may be defensive rather than opportunity-seeking
- Timing may be slower — prefers certainty over speed
- Impact may be steady but not transformative

**Development Focus:** Develop market opportunity orientation alongside risk awareness; build timing judgment for when to move beyond stability; develop impact ambition beyond maintaining the status quo.

**Best-Fit Context:** Crisis management; stability and turnaround situations; risk-intensive environments; governance and control roles.

### A6 — Steward
**Dominant Pair:** Capability × Impact  

**Core Pattern:** Develops and protects organisational capability over time, with measurable long-term impact

**Strengths:**
- Deep capability development and protection orientation
- Long-term impact orientation — builds for durability
- Strong at developing and preserving organisational capability
- Steady, reliable contribution over extended periods

**Growth Areas:**
- May be overly protective — resists change that could erode capability
- Market responsiveness may be slower — prioritises capability integrity over market adaptation
- Timing may be slower — prefers proven paths over novel opportunities
- Risk comfort may be low — prefer stability to volatility

**Development Focus:** Develop market responsiveness alongside capability protection; build adaptive capacity for changing contexts; develop risk comfort for necessary change.

**Best-Fit Context:** Institutional capability preservation; long-term talent development; knowledge-intensive organisations; stewardship of legacy capabilities.

### A7 — Cultivator
**Dominant Pair:** Capability × Market  

**Core Pattern:** Grows capability aligned to market evolution — adapts capabilities as market shifts

**Strengths:**
- Strong alignment between capability development and market direction
- Grows capabilities in response to market signals
- Good at translating market changes into capability priorities
- Adaptive capability development — builds what the market will value

**Growth Areas:**
- Timing precision may be moderate — grows with market but not necessarily ahead of it
- Risk orientation may be moderate — prefers incremental to transformative
- Impact scale may be limited — strong at growth, less so at step-change
- May follow market rather than shape it

**Development Focus:** Develop timing judgment to move ahead of market shifts; build risk comfort for transformative moves; develop impact scaling for larger organisational change.

**Best-Fit Context:** Capability development aligned to market evolution; talent development programmes; organisational learning and adaptation; gradual capability transformation.

### A8 — Guardian
**Dominant Pair:** Risk × Market  

**Core Pattern:** Maintains positioning integrity under competitive pressure — protects market position with disciplined risk management

**Strengths:**
- Deep market understanding and strong risk discipline
- Protects market position effectively under competitive pressure
- Good at identifying and mitigating competitive threats
- Maintains positioning integrity through market turbulence

**Growth Areas:**
- May be more defensive than offensive — protects position but doesn't aggressively expand
- Timing may be conservative — waits for clarity before moving
- Capability depth may be narrower — strong on market, thinner on building
- Risk of over-protecting — missing growth opportunities

**Development Focus:** Develop offensive orientation alongside defensive strength; build capability for expansion; develop timing instinct for counter-strikes and opportunistic moves.

**Best-Fit Context:** Market position defence; competitive intelligence; risk and compliance leadership; protecting established positions in competitive markets.

### A9 — Diplomat
**Dominant Pair:** Impact × Risk  

**Core Pattern:** Navigates complex stakeholder environments with precision — delivers impact while managing risk

**Strengths:**
- Strong impact orientation paired with sophisticated risk awareness
- Delivers results in complex stakeholder environments
- Good at influence without formal authority
- Manages political and relational risk effectively

**Growth Areas:**
- Market analysis may be secondary to stakeholder management
- Capability depth may be thinner — strong on influence, weaker on substantive expertise depth
- Timing may be reactive to stakeholder dynamics rather than market timing
- Risk of over-calibrating to stakeholders at the expense of market positioning

**Development Focus:** Deepen market analysis capability; build substantive expertise alongside stakeholder skill; develop market-driven timing alongside stakeholder timing.

**Best-Fit Context:** Stakeholder management; matrix and cross-organisational leadership; regulatory and government relations; complex multi-party environments.

### A10 — Convener
**Dominant Pair:** Market × Impact  

**Core Pattern:** Creates alignment across different market and cultural contexts — brings people together around market opportunities

**Strengths:**
- Strong market understanding paired with broad impact scope
- Creates alignment across diverse stakeholder and cultural contexts
- Communicates market insight in ways that resonate across groups
- Builds coalitions around market opportunities

**Growth Areas:**
- Capability depth may be thinner — strong on connecting, weaker on substantive depth
- Risk assessment may be underdeveloped — focus on alignment may overlook risk factors
- Timing may be consensus-dependent rather than anticipatory
- May prioritise alignment over speed

**Development Focus:** Build substantive capability depth alongside convening skill; develop risk assessment for aligned initiatives; develop timing independence from consensus cycles.

**Best-Fit Context:** Cross-organisational alignment; market opportunity convening; multi-stakeholder initiatives; partnership and alliance building.

### A11 — Translator
**Dominant Pair:** Capability × Market  

**Core Pattern:** Bridges capability gaps between different organisational worlds — translates market needs into capability language and back

**Strengths:**
- Deep understanding of both market dynamics and capability realities
- Bridges capability gaps between different organisational units or cultures
- Translates market needs into capability development priorities
- Helps different parts of the organisation understand each other

**Growth Areas:**
- Impact may be indirect — strong on bridging, but results may be diffuse
- Risk orientation may be moderate — bridges gaps but may not drive bold change
- Timing may be responsive rather than anticipatory
- May be valued as a bridge but not as a leader of substantive change

**Development Focus:** Develop more direct impact orientation — move beyond bridging to driving; build risk comfort for substantive change; develop timing judgment for when to push versus when to bridge.

**Best-Fit Context:** Cross-functional or cross-cultural bridges; capability translation between teams; integration roles; change management that requires deep understanding of both sides.

### A12 — Champion
**Dominant Pair:** Impact × Timing  

**Core Pattern:** Advocates powerfully at the right moment — delivers impact with precise timing

**Strengths:**
- Strong impact capability paired with excellent timing judgment
- Knows when to push and when to hold on advocacy
- Delivers results at the optimal moment
- Powerful advocate — knows when the window is open

**Growth Areas:**
- Market depth may be thinner — strong on seizing moments, weaker on sustained market analysis
- Capability depth may be moderate — strong on advocacy, may be lighter on substantive expertise
- Risk assessment may be underdeveloped — timing confidence can lead to risk underestimation
- Impact may peak around specific moments rather than being sustained

**Development Focus:** Deepen market analysis to validate timing judgments; build substantive capability alongside advocacy skill; develop risk assessment for high-impact timing moves.

**Best-Fit Context:** Advocacy and influence roles; market window opportunities; campaign leadership; stakeholder impact at critical moments.

### A13 — Analyst
**Dominant Pair:** Market × Risk  

**Core Pattern:** Deconstructs competitive complexity into actionable intelligence — thorough market analysis with risk awareness

**Strengths:**
- Deep market analysis and competitive intelligence capability
- Strong risk awareness and assessment discipline
- Deconstructs complex market situations into clear insights
- Identifies risks that less analytical leaders miss

**Growth Areas:**
- May be more analytical than action-oriented — strong insights, slower to act
- Impact may be advisory rather than direct — informs decisions rather than driving them
- Capability depth may be analytical rather than operational
- Risk of analysis paralysis — thorough but slow

**Development Focus:** Develop action orientation alongside analysis depth; build impact delivery capability; develop timing judgment for when analysis is sufficient and action should begin.

**Best-Fit Context:** Market research and analysis; competitive intelligence; strategy formulation; risk assessment and planning.

### A14 — Visionary
**Dominant Pair:** Timing × Impact  

**Core Pattern:** Sees the future positioning others have not yet recognised — combines timing foresight with impact ambition

**Strengths:**
- Exceptional timing foresight — sees future positioning before others
- Strong impact ambition — oriented toward significant results
- Inspiring vision of what could be
- Combines future orientation with delivery ambition

**Growth Areas:**
- Capability depth may not match the vision — strong on what could be, weaker on how to get there
- Market analysis may be intuitive rather than systematic — right direction but less precise on details
- Risk assessment may be underdeveloped — vision confidence can underestimate obstacles
- Risk of the vision outpacing the execution capability

**Development Focus:** Build capability depth to support the vision; develop systematic market analysis to validate intuitive foresight; develop risk assessment and mitigation for ambitious future moves.

**Best-Fit Context:** Vision and direction roles; future positioning; long-range strategy; inspirational leadership; market creation.

## 4. Interpretation Framework

### Composite Score Interpretation

| Band | Score Range | Interpretation |
|------|-------------|----------------|
| Exceptional | 85–100 | High competitive positioning readiness across all five dimensions; archetypal primary archetype activation. |
| Strong | 70–84 | Strong readiness with one or two targeted development dimensions. |
| Developing | 55–69 | Solid foundation; specific capability and positioning gaps present. |
| Emerging | 40–54 | Foundational positioning work required; recommend structured development before next mandate. |
| Early Stage | 0–39 | Significant gaps across multiple dimensions; developmental programme recommended. |

### Dimension-Level Interpretation

- **Strong Primary (17–20):** Dimension is a clear, deployable leadership strength; anchors the leader's archetype profile
- **Strong Secondary (13–16):** Dimension is accessible and effective; available as a secondary mode under deliberate activation
- **Reserve (9–12):** Dimension is present but inconsistently deployed; available under favourable conditions
- **Weak (5–8):** Dimension is underdeveloped; creates friction in contexts that require it; development priority
- **Out of Scope (0–4):** Dimension is absent or counterproductive; not available for deployment in current form
