## 1. Model Explanation

A 36-item motivational assessment with 30 core motivation items and 6 engagement risk items. DRIVE measures motivation architecture across five dimensions (Intrinsic Motivation, Extrinsic Motivation, Values Alignment, Confidence & Self-Efficacy, Growth Orientation) and classifies individuals into 10 motivational profiles across three engagement states (Fueled, Flickering, Fading).

The assessment measures 5 dimensions with 36 items on a 5-point Likert (1 = Strongly Disagree, 5 = Strongly Agree) scale. Each dimension is normalised to a 0–20 score, and the composite is expressed as a 0–100 percentile.

The archetype system classifies leaders into 10 archetypes based on Archetypes are determined by dominant motivation type (Intrinsic, Extrinsic, Purpose-driven, Growth-driven, Dual-drive, or Depleted/Blocked) combined with engagement state (Fueled, Flickering, Fading). Primary classification uses D1 vs D2 dominance, with D3 (Values) and D5 (Growth) as override conditions..

## 3. Archetype Detail

### A1 — Achiever
**Motivation Type:** Extrinsic-dominant  
**Engagement State:** Fueled  

**Core Pattern:** 

**Strengths:**
- Results-oriented and high-output
- Strong drive for progression and visibility
- High confidence in their capabilities
- Growth-oriented — actively develops for advancement

**Growth Areas:**
- Risk of burning out when recognition dries up
- May prioritise visible wins over deeper, less visible value creation
- Intrinsic motivation may be thinner than it appears
- Vulnerable to environments with unclear reward structures

**Development Focus:** Strengthen intrinsic motivation for sustainable engagement; develop appreciation for non-visible value creation; build recognition-independent performance standards.

### A2 — Craftsman
**Motivation Type:** Intrinsic-dominant  
**Engagement State:** Fueled  

**Core Pattern:** 

**Strengths:**
- Deep work satisfaction independent of external rewards
- Commitment to quality and craftsmanship
- Strong values alignment with their work
- Sustainable engagement through intrinsic satisfaction

**Growth Areas:**
- May be demotivated by bureaucracy and mediocrity
- Can be slow to advance because less driven by visibility and recognition
- May undervalue their own commercial impact
- Risk of becoming complacent in stable environments

**Development Focus:** Build external orientation — stakeholder visibility, commercial impact awareness; develop career advancement strategy aligned with intrinsic values; practice communicating value in ways that resonate with extrinsic systems.

### A3 — Champion
**Motivation Type:** Relational-dominant  
**Engagement State:** Fueled  

**Core Pattern:** 

**Strengths:**
- Strong people development orientation
- High confidence that inspires others
- Growth-oriented — both for self and team
- Recognition from team success fuels sustained effort

**Growth Areas:**
- Loses motivation when team structure changes or team is removed
- May prioritise team harmony over individual performance standards
- Risk of over-investing in low-potential team members
- Personal identity may be too tied to team role

**Development Focus:** Build independent motivational foundations not solely dependent on team role; develop individual performance management capability; practice maintaining motivation during team transitions.

### A4 — Explorer
**Motivation Type:** Intrinsic-dominant  
**Engagement State:** Fueled  

**Core Pattern:** 

**Strengths:**
- Thrives in new and challenging situations
- Rapid learning capability
- Comfortable with ambiguity and change
- Strong growth orientation

**Growth Areas:**
- Disengages in stable, routine mandates
- May move on before mastering or seeing through long-term initiatives
- Risk of breadth without depth
- Can become restless in roles with long plateau phases

**Development Focus:** Build stamina for the mastery phase of roles; develop satisfaction from deepening expertise rather than just new challenges; identify how to bring exploratory energy to stable contexts.

### A5 — Stalwart
**Motivation Type:** Purpose-driven  
**Engagement State:** Fueled  

**Core Pattern:** 

**Strengths:**
- Deep institutional loyalty and commitment
- Strong values alignment with the organisation
- Reliable and consistent over long periods
- Custodians of organisational culture and knowledge

**Growth Areas:**
- Can be rigid in changing contexts
- Lower growth orientation may mean slower adaptation to new requirements
- May resist organisational change that disrupts familiar patterns
- Risk of becoming outdated if development needs accelerate

**Development Focus:** Build openness to change and growth; develop adaptive capacity for shifting contexts; connect growth and development to values and purpose rather than seeing it as disruption.

### A6 — Restless
**Motivation Type:** Growth-driven  
**Engagement State:** Flickering  

**Core Pattern:** 

**Strengths:**
- High capability and ambition
- Strong growth orientation
- Driven by progression and recognition
- Clear about what they want — advancement

**Growth Areas:**
- Will exit if mandate does not expand or values alignment does not improve
- May disengage incrementally — still performing but not at full capacity
- Risk of looking for opportunities externally before internal options are exhausted
- Values misalignment creates background dissatisfaction that affects long-term retention

**Development Focus:** Clarify values alignment gap — is it role-specific or organisational? Explore growth opportunities that increase values congruence; assess whether role scope can expand to match capability and ambition.

### A7 — Golden Handcuffs
**Motivation Type:** Extrinsic-dominant  
**Engagement State:** Flickering  

**Core Pattern:** 

**Strengths:**
- Reliable performer — will meet expectations
- Values alignment prevents active disengagement
- Understands the reward system and works within it effectively
- Stable presence in the organisation

**Growth Areas:**
- Explore what would need to change to re-engage intrinsic motivation
- Risk of prolonged mediocre performance — showing up but not contributing at full capacity
- May stay far longer than is healthy for their development
- Vulnerable to being passed over for more visibly motivated peers

**Development Focus:** Identify what would re-engage intrinsic motivation — is it role content, autonomy, challenge, or something else? Assess whether staying is serving long-term career interests or just short-term comfort.

### A8 — Drifter
**Motivation Type:** No clear dominant  
**Engagement State:** Flickering  

**Core Pattern:** 

**Strengths:**
- Often capable — the issue is motivation, not ability
- May be in a transition period between motivational phases
- Flexible — not locked into one motivational pattern
- Open to discovering what drives them

**Growth Areas:**
- Mandates feel arbitrary and unmoored from personal motivation
- Performance is unpredictable — dependent on whether the work happens to engage them
- Risk of prolonged underperformance without intervention
- May not have developed self-awareness about what actually motivates them

**Development Focus:** Conduct systematic motivational exploration — test different types of work, goals, and rewards to identify what creates sustained engagement; work with a coach to develop motivational self-awareness.

### A9 — Burned-Out
**Motivation Type:** Depleted  
**Engagement State:** Fading  

**Core Pattern:** 

**Strengths:**
- Often were high performers before depletion set in
- Recovery is possible with the right intervention
- Typically have deep capability and institutional knowledge
- Recovery often leads to renewed clarity about what matters

**Growth Areas:**
- Immediate safety assessment required — risk of further deterioration
- Confidence collapse affects both performance and help-seeking
- Intrinsic motivation depletion means the work itself no longer provides reward
- Growth orientation collapse means they cannot imagine things getting better

**Development Focus:** Immediate intervention: workload assessment, burnout recovery plan, possible role change or sabbatical. Focus on safety and recovery first, then rebuilding motivation. Professional support (therapy, coaching) is strongly indicated.

### A10 — Frozen Asset
**Motivation Type:** Blocked  
**Engagement State:** Fading  

**Core Pattern:** 

**Strengths:**
- Strong values alignment — genuinely committed to the organisation's mission
- Often significant untapped capability beneath the paralysis
- Loyal and stable — will not leave easily
- Recovery can release substantial value for the organisation

**Growth Areas:**
- Organisational or mandate design intervention may be required
- Low confidence creates self-reinforcing paralysis — less action, less confidence
- Low growth orientation means they may not actively seek to change their situation
- Risk of prolonged underutilisation, which gradually erodes capability further

**Development Focus:** Structural intervention — role redesign, mandate clarification, or targeted development programme. Build confidence through small wins and increasing autonomy. Connect growth opportunities to values alignment to increase motivation for change.

## 4. Interpretation Framework

### Composite Score Interpretation

| Band | Score Range | Interpretation |
|------|-------------|----------------|
| High Drive | 80–100 | Strong motivation across multiple dimensions; highly engaged and directed. |
| Active Drive | 60–79 | Generally energised; 1–2 motivation dimensions with meaningful gaps. |
| Moderate Drive | 40–59 | Partial alignment; significant gaps between role and motivational architecture. |
| Low Drive | 20–39 | Substantial misalignment; disengagement risk is material. |
| Disengaged | 0–19 | Motivational breakdown; urgent career review and role redesign required. |

### Dimension-Level Interpretation

- **Strong (16–20):** Mature motivation architecture with sustainable engagement
- **Developing (10–15.9):** Moderate motivation with identifiable risk factors
- **Gap (0–9.9):** Significant motivation architecture gap requiring intervention
