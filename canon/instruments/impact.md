# IMPACT — Deep Canon
**board & stakeholder impact**

---

## Model Explanation

IMPACT is a composite SHIFT assessment that measures an individual's ability to create influence and impact at the board and senior stakeholder level. It is the flagship product of the P3 Strategic & Organizational Impact pillar — the highest-level assessment in the portfolio, focused on the most senior leaders.

The model uses a 30-question self-report survey (6 per dimension) on a 5-point Likert scale. Outputs include a five-dimension profile, an impact archetype (one of eight), and a development roadmap for increasing board-level and stakeholder influence.

The eight archetypes sit on a structural grid defined by two axes: **Strategic Oversight depth** (vertical: operational → strategic) and **Stakeholder Intelligence breadth** (horizontal: internal → external). These two axes define the positioning landscape — they are NOT archetypes themselves.

**Important:** The current source code lists 10 "archetypes" but 2 of them (Axis 1, Axis 2) are actually the grid axes, not person archetypes. The canon count is 8 archetypes.

---

## Dimension Interrelationships

The five dimensions measure different aspects of board/stakeholder impact:

**Strategic ↔ Operational axis:**
- Strategic Oversight (vision, direction, long-term)
- Governance Rigour (discipline, fiduciary, risk)

**Internal ↔ External axis:**
- Stakeholder Intelligence (mapping, relationships, external)
- Mandate Legacy (enduring contribution, institutional)

**Individual capability layer:**
- Executive Presence & Influence (personal credibility, presence, impact delivery)

**Key interaction patterns:**
- High Strategic Oversight + High Stakeholder Intelligence = Visionary archetype
- High Governance Rigour + High Mandate Legacy = Guardian archetype
- High Stakeholder Intelligence + High Executive Presence = Networker archetype
- Low across the board = Passenger archetype

## Archetype Detail

### The Steward
**Primary dimensions:** Strategic Oversight + Governance Rigour

Disciplined strategic leader who balances long-term vision with rigorous governance and fiduciary responsibility. The Steward takes their oversight role seriously — they know the numbers, they understand the risks, and they ensure the organization is well-governed.

**Strengths:** Governance expertise, strategic discipline, fiduciary focus, institutional memory
**Growth areas:** Stakeholder outreach — can be too internally focused; need to build external relationships and influence
**Board role fit:** Audit committee chair, risk committee, governance-focused board member

---

### The Networker
**Primary dimensions:** Stakeholder Intelligence + Executive Presence

Charismatic connector with extensive stakeholder relationships and a commanding presence in board and executive circles. Doors open when The Networker walks in — people know them, people take their calls.

**Strengths:** Relationship building, influence, visibility, access, deal-making ability
**Growth areas:** Substance and governance depth — can be perceived as "all surface, no depth"; need to build substantive credibility
**Board role fit:** Nominating/governance, external relations, board member with strong network leverage

---

### The Guardian
**Primary dimensions:** Governance Rigour + Mandate Legacy

Principle-driven leader who protects organizational integrity and builds institutions that outlast their tenure. The Guardian is motivated by doing the right thing — for the organization, for shareholders, for the long term.

**Strengths:** Integrity, institution building, long-term perspective, ethical leadership
**Growth areas:** Adaptability — can be too conservative in fast-changing environments; risk of being seen as a brake on progress
**Board role fit:** Board chair, governance committee, ethics/compliance oversight

---

### The Visionary
**Primary dimensions:** Strategic Oversight + Stakeholder Intelligence

Forward-thinking leader who sees around corners and builds coalitions of stakeholders around a compelling future vision. The Visionary can paint a picture of the future that makes people want to follow — and they know how to get the right people in the room.

**Strengths:** Strategic foresight, coalition building, inspirational leadership, market insight
**Growth areas:** Governance discipline and operational follow-through — big vision can overlook details and compliance
**Board role fit:** Strategy committee chair, board member driving transformation, M&A and growth initiatives

---

### The Bridge-Builder
**Primary dimensions:** Stakeholder Intelligence + Strategic Oversight (both medium-high)

Collaborative leader who connects different stakeholder groups, builds consensus, and drives alignment across diverse interests. The Bridge-Builder is the person you want in the room when there are competing agendas — they find common ground.

**Strengths:** Consensus building, cross-group alignment, relationship brokerage, diplomacy
**Growth areas:** Decisiveness — can over-prioritize harmony over action; need to know when to stop building bridges and start making decisions
**Board role fit:** Mediating board conflicts, stakeholder engagement, cross-committee coordination

---

### The Nominee
**Primary dimensions:** Medium across the board — formal authority, growing influence

Board-appointed or external-candidate leader with solid credentials but still building deep organizational influence and relationships. The Nominee has the title and the CV — but hasn't yet built the genuine relationships and institutional knowledge that create real board impact.

**Strengths:** Fresh perspective, external expertise, formal authority, credentialed
**Growth areas:** Organizational integration, relationship building, moving from "appointed" to "influential"
**Board role fit:** New board members, external appointments, early-stage board careers

---

### The Passenger
**Primary dimensions:** Low across all dimensions

Leader at the board or executive level who is present but not driving impact. They attend meetings, they vote, they show up — but they're not shaping outcomes, building relationships, or contributing strategically.

**Strengths:** Low risk, stable presence, reliable attendance
**Growth areas:** Everything — active engagement, strategic contribution, relationship building, value creation
**Board role fit:** Needs development — not currently contributing at expected board level

---

### The Strategic Builder
**Primary dimensions:** Strategic Oversight + Mandate Legacy

Transformation-focused leader who leaves a legacy of structural change — builds institutions, reshapes strategies, and creates lasting organizational value. The Strategic Builder doesn't just serve on a board — they leave the organization better than they found it.

**Strengths:** Strategic transformation, legacy building, institutional change, value creation
**Growth areas:** Stakeholder management during transformation — can leave people behind when driving big changes; need to bring stakeholders along
**Board role fit:** Board chair during turnaround/transformation, strategy + governance combined roles, founder-director hybrid

## Interpretation Framework

**Score interpretation:**
1. **Archetype first** — which impact pattern dominates?
2. **Dimension profile** — where are the specific strengths and gaps?
3. **Axis positioning** — where do they sit on the strategic↔operational and internal↔external axes?
4. **Development priorities** — 2-3 highest-impact areas for growth

**Board readiness indicator:**
- High Strategic Oversight + High Governance Rigour = board-ready (Steward / Guardian)
- High Stakeholder Intelligence + High Executive Presence = board-accessible (Networker)
- Balanced high scores across all dimensions = board-excellent (Visionary / Strategic Builder)
- Low across the board = board-developing (Nominee / Passenger)

---

## Edge Cases

**All dimensions very high (Strategic Builder / Visionary):**
- These are the highest-impact board members
- Risk: over-confidence or over-extension
- Development focus: stakeholder care during transformation and legacy building

**All dimensions very low (Passenger):**
- Important to frame as developmental opportunity, not criticism
- Start with one or two focus areas rather than overwhelming
- Governance basics + stakeholder relationship building are good starting points

**High Strategic Oversight, Low Stakeholder Intelligence (Steward leaning inward):**
- Excellent on substance, limited on influence
- Development: stakeholder mapping and relationship building

**High Stakeholder Intelligence, Low Governance Rigour (Networker without substance):**
- Great access, limited credibility
- Development: governance fundamentals and substantive domain expertise

---

## Banned Patterns — IMPACT Specific

1. **Never call it "Board Effectiveness Assessment"** — IMPACT is broader than board effectiveness; it's about stakeholder impact at all senior levels.
2. **Never count Axis 1/Axis 2 as archetypes** — they're the grid, not archetypes.
3. **Never use "Architect" as an archetype name** — banned word; use "Strategic Builder" instead.
4. **Never over-claim APAC specificity** — IMPACT D5 is about executive presence generally, not specifically "APAC Mandate Credibility" (that's BRIDGE's territory).
5. **Never present it as a board evaluation tool** — it's an individual impact assessment; organizational board effectiveness is a different product.
