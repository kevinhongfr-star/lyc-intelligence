# CPI — Deep Canon
**China Leadership Pipeline Index**

---

## Model Explanation

CPI is a B2B organizational diagnostic that measures the health of a company's gender-balanced leadership pipeline across six weighted dimensions. It is the flagship product of the P1 Talent Pipeline Health pillar and the overall LYC Intelligence portfolio.

The model works on a dual-tier architecture: a 12-question Lite version (2 questions per dimension) and a 36-question Full version (6 questions per dimension). Both share the same scoring engine, the same six dimensions, and the same archetype system — the Lite version is just a faster, more accessible entry point.

The output is a composite CPI score (0-100, percentage representation equivalent), a tier classification (Tier 1-4), a specific pipeline archetype (one of six), benchmark comparison against a 200-company China database, and a priority-ranked action roadmap.

---

## Dimension Interrelationships

The six dimensions are not independent — they form a system:

**Input → Throughput → Output cascade:**
- D6 (External Hiring Capability) and D3 (Development Investment) are *input/process* dimensions
- D2 (Pipeline Depth) is the *throughput* dimension
- D1 (Talent Representation) is the *output* dimension
- D4 (Cultural Alignment) is the *enabling environment* that affects all others
- D5 (Retention Risk) is the *leakage* dimension that drains the pipeline at every stage

**Key interaction patterns:**
- High D3 + Low D1 = Glass Ceiling (development exists but doesn't reach the top)
- High D1 + Low D2 = Shallow Bench (top looks good but nothing underneath)
- High D1+D2 + Low D4 = Cultural Mismatch (structure without substance)
- High D2+D3 + Low D5 = Leaky Bucket (building the pipeline but losing people)
- High D6 + Low D2 = External Dependency (buying talent instead of building it)

## Archetype Detail

### A1 — The Glass Ceiling
**Trigger:** High Development Investment (≥60%), Low Talent Representation (≤40%), gap ≥ 20 points

The organization develops women well through middle management, but a clear ceiling blocks advancement to the executive layer. This is the most common archetype among MNCs in China — strong L&D infrastructure and genuine intent, but structural and cultural barriers at the top prevent final breakthrough.

**Strengths:**
- Strong internal development infrastructure and velocity
- Healthy mid-level pipeline
- Active managerial development of women

**Blind spots:**
- Invisible barriers at the executive layer
- Succession slates lacking women for top roles
- Sponsorship gaps at C-suite level
- P&L and critical experience gaps at the final promotion gate

**Primary intervention:** Executive Sponsorship Mandate — pair each high-potential senior woman with an ExCo sponsor who advocates for her placement in top roles, with accountability for outcomes.

---

### A2 — Shallow Bench
**Trigger:** High Talent Representation (≥60%), Low Pipeline Depth (≤40%), gap ≥ 20 points

Top-level representation is visible — often through senior hires or early breakthroughs — but the pipeline beneath is thin. The organization is one or two departures away from losing its representation gains.

**Strengths:**
- Visible senior-level commitment and representation
- Role models exist at the top
- Leadership credibility on gender diversity

**Blind spots:**
- Pipeline fragility — losing 1-2 senior women would erode gains significantly
- Underinvestment in mid-level talent identification
- Risk of "token" perception
- Succession risk for critical roles with no diverse ready-now candidates

**Primary intervention:** Mid-Level Talent Accelerator — structured high-potential identification and acceleration at manager-to-director level, targeting 2x increase in ready-now women candidates within 18 months.

---

### A3 — Cultural Mismatch
**Trigger:** High Representation + Pipeline Depth (both ≥60%), Low Cultural Alignment (≤45%), gap ≥ 15 points

The numbers look good on paper, but day-to-day experience doesn't match. Women are present but may not fully participate, contribute, or feel they belong. This is the most insidious archetype because structural metrics mask a deeper engagement and belonging deficit.

**Strengths:**
- Strong representation numbers and pipeline infrastructure
- Formal programs and processes exist
- Visible leadership commitment on paper

**Blind spots:**
- Day-to-day experience doesn't match formal diversity narrative
- Psychological safety gaps in meetings and decision-making
- Unaddressed microaggressions and exclusionary behaviors
- High potential for sudden retention crisis when cultural fatigue hits
- Risk of "performative" DEI perception

**Primary intervention:** Inclusion Pulse + Targeted Action — confidential pulse survey focused on psychological safety, fairness of evaluation, and sense of belonging, with transparent results and targeted workstreams.

---

### A4 — Leaky Bucket
**Trigger:** High Pipeline Depth + Development Investment (both ≥60%), Low Retention (≤45%), gap ≥ 15 points

The organization effectively develops and advances women through the pipeline, but loses them at disproportionate rates — particularly at mid-career, post-maternity, or senior leadership inflection points.

**Strengths:**
- Strong development systems and promotion velocity
- Women join and progress well in early-to-mid career
- Attractive employer value proposition for early-career women

**Blind spots:**
- ROI leakage from lost development investment
- Mid-career and senior talent drain to competitors
- Caregiving and flexibility policy gaps
- Cultural factors visible only after senior promotion
- Cumulative effect of micro-exclusions driving departure

**Primary intervention:** Retention Diagnostic + Stay Interviews — structured stay interviews with high-potential women at all levels, segmented by career stage, function, and tenure.

---

### A5 — External Dependency
**Trigger:** High External Hiring Capability (≥60%), Low Pipeline Depth (≤40%), gap ≥ 20 points

Senior-level gender gaps are filled primarily through external hiring rather than internal development. Quick representation wins, but unsustainable and expensive.

**Strengths:**
- Ability to attract senior women talent from the market
- Quick progress on representation through targeted hiring
- Fresh thinking from external hires

**Blind spots:**
- High cost and risk of executive external hires
- Weak signal to internal women — they may leave to advance elsewhere
- Cultural integration challenges
- No sustainable pipeline — representation depends on market availability
- Risk of "hire and depart" cycle

**Primary intervention:** Internal Promotions KPI Shift — set a target for internal fill rate of senior roles (e.g., 70%+), hold business leaders accountable, and tie incentives to internal development metrics.

---

### A6 — World-Class Pipeline
**Trigger:** All dimensions ≥ 70th percentile

Excellence across all six dimensions. Rare, well-deserved, and fragile if not maintained.

**Strengths:**
- Balanced excellence across all six dimensions
- Sustainable representation with deep internal bench
- Strong cultural foundation
- Employer brand advantage

**Blind spots:**
- Complacency risk — "we've arrived" mentality
- Market and organizational shifts can disrupt balance quickly
- Succession risk if not continuously investing in next tier
- Potential backlash if progress seen as "enough" without broader inclusion

**Primary intervention:** Intersectionality Expansion — move beyond binary gender to address race, age, disability, LGBTQ+ intersectionality.

---

### A0 — Balanced Profile (Fallback)
**Trigger:** No single archetype pattern meets threshold criteria

Scores are relatively consistent across dimensions — either an early-stage organization with uniform gaps, or one working on multiple fronts simultaneously. This is a residual category, not a product-counted archetype.

## Interpretation Framework

**Score interpretation hierarchy:**
1. **Tier placement** — where the organization sits relative to benchmark (Tier 1-4)
2. **Archetype** — the dominant pattern that defines the pipeline challenge
3. **Dimension gaps** — which specific dimensions are driving the archetype
4. **Industry comparison** — how the score stacks against direct competitors
5. **Action priorities** — what to do first, second, third

**Report structure:**
- Executive summary (tier + score + archetype + headline insight)
- Dimension-by-dimension breakdown with interpretation prose
- Archetype deep dive (pattern, strengths, blind spots, actions)
- Benchmark comparison (overall + by industry)
- Prioritized action roadmap (3 priorities, ranked by impact)

## Edge Cases

**All dimensions very low (Tier 4):**
- Will likely map to Balanced Profile (A0) since no pattern dominates
- This is correct — early-stage organizations need a foundation-building strategy, not a targeted archetype fix

**Two archetypes triggering simultaneously:**
- Engine selects based on highest confidence score and pattern match strength
- Edge cases are flagged with "medium" confidence in the report
- Consultant should use judgment to confirm the archetype that resonates most with the client

**D6 (External Hiring) as the highest dimension:**
- Counterintuitively, high external hiring is NOT always a strength
- If D6 is high but D2 is low, that's External Dependency — a structural weakness
- D6 is only a strength when paired with healthy D2 and D1

**Cultural Alignment (D4) as the weakest dimension:**
- When D4 is low but everything else is high, it's Cultural Mismatch (A3)
- This is the most important archetype to identify early — it predicts future retention crises
- Organizations with A3 often think they're doing well until the culture catches up

## Banned Patterns — CPI Specific

1. **Never call it "Career Positioning Index"** — CPI is China Leadership Pipeline Index, B2B organizational, not individual career.
2. **Never present it as an individual assessment** — it's organizational-level.
3. **Never use "benchmark" to imply competitive ranking** — it's a developmental benchmark, not a contest. (Note: "benchmark" is technically on the banned words list but is acceptable in the specific context of "industry benchmark data" — verify with ECHO before using in marketing copy.)
4. **Never promise specific representation improvements** — the diagnostic identifies patterns, it doesn't guarantee outcomes.
5. **Never present the archetypes as "personality types"** — they're organizational pipeline patterns, not individual traits.
