## 1. Model Explanation

A 24-item assessment that measures a leader's coaching capability in bilateral (cross-boundary) contexts across four dimensions: developmental orientation, adaptive coaching style, bilateral relationship quality, and coaching under constraints. It evaluates whether leaders can develop talent effectively across organisational and cultural boundaries, not just within their own teams.

The assessment measures 4 dimensions with 24 items on a 5-point Likert (1 = Strongly Disagree, 5 = Strongly Agree) scale. Each dimension is normalised to a 0–20 score, and the composite is expressed as a 0–100 percentile.

The archetype system classifies leaders into 5 archetypes based on Archetypes are defined by which dimensions score in the Strong range (16+/20). Combinations of strong dimensions define different coaching capability profiles. The model captures both the breadth (how many dimensions are strong) and the specific pattern of strength..

## 3. Archetype Detail

### A1 — The Bilateral Developer
**Strength Dimensions:** Cross-Boundary Developmental Orientation, Adaptive Coaching Style, Bilateral Developmental Relationship Quality  

**Core Pattern:** Fully developed bilateral coaching capability. Maintains developmental orientation across contexts, adapts coaching style effectively, and builds deep trust with counterparts.

**Strengths:**
- Sees cross-boundary development as core responsibility, not optional
- Adapts coaching style to person, context, and culture
- Builds deep developmental trust across organisational and cultural boundaries
- Creates lasting developmental relationships that outlast transactions

**Growth Areas:**
- Coaching under constraints may be the weakest dimension — may need pressure-management strategies
- May over-invest in development even when performance pressure requires faster results
- Risk of blurring appropriate boundaries between developmental and accountability relationships

**Development Focus:** Strengthen coaching under pressure and authority ambiguity; develop boundary-setting between developmental support and performance accountability; build pressure-management tactics that preserve coaching stance.

**Organisational Impact:** Can develop leadership talent in any bilateral context. Creates lasting developmental relationships that outlast specific transactions.

### A2 — The Adaptive Coach
**Strength Dimensions:** Adaptive Coaching Style, Coaching Under Bilateral Constraints  

**Core Pattern:** Can adapt coaching approach to context and maintain coaching stance under pressure. May not invest as deeply in developmental orientation or relationship building, but effective in volatile contexts.

**Strengths:**
- Versatile coaching toolkit — multiple approaches for different contexts
- Maintains coaching stance under pressure and ambiguity
- Effective in volatile or rapidly changing bilateral environments
- Culturally adaptive feedback delivery

**Growth Areas:**
- Developmental orientation may be thinner — coaches effectively but doesn't necessarily see it as core responsibility
- Developmental relationship depth may be limited — effective but not necessarily deep
- May be more technique-driven than purpose-driven

**Development Focus:** Deepen developmental orientation — connect coaching to broader talent development purpose; build deeper developmental relationship investment; develop relationship-building patience alongside technique.

**Organisational Impact:** Valuable in contexts where coaching approaches must shift rapidly. Provides coaching continuity during organisational disruption.

### A3 — The Trust Builder
**Strength Dimensions:** Bilateral Developmental Relationship Quality, Cross-Boundary Developmental Orientation  

**Core Pattern:** Builds strong developmental relationships and maintains genuine developmental orientation. May be less adaptive in coaching style and struggle under bilateral constraints.

**Strengths:**
- Builds deep developmental trust across boundaries
- Sees cross-boundary development as core responsibility
- Strong at accountability within relationship context
- Effective re-engagement strategies for off-track development

**Growth Areas:**
- Coaching style may be less adaptive — uses a consistent approach across different people and contexts
- May struggle to maintain coaching stance under pressure
- Effectiveness may drop in high-constraint or authority-ambiguous situations

**Development Focus:** Build adaptive coaching techniques — develop multiple approaches for different developmental needs; build coaching-under-pressure strategies; develop comfort with authority ambiguity.

**Organisational Impact:** Creates deep developmental relationships. Most effective in stable bilateral contexts where trust can develop over time.

### A4 — The Pressure-Tested Coach
**Strength Dimensions:** Coaching Under Bilateral Constraints  

**Core Pattern:** Can maintain coaching stance under pressure but may lack the developmental orientation, adaptive style, or relationship depth for comprehensive bilateral development.

**Strengths:**
- Reliable under pressure — doesn't default to telling when stressed
- Comfortable with authority ambiguity
- Strong self-awareness about coaching trigger points
- Effective in constrained or difficult bilateral situations

**Growth Areas:**
- Developmental orientation may be limited — coaches effectively under pressure but doesn't proactively seek development opportunities
- Coaching style may be narrow — one primary approach rather than adaptive repertoire
- Relationship depth may be functional rather than developmental

**Development Focus:** Build developmental orientation — expand from 'can coach when needed' to 'actively develops talent across boundaries'; build adaptive coaching style repertoire; deepen developmental relationship investment.

**Organisational Impact:** Reliable under pressure but limited in scope. Needs complementary coaching capabilities in the team.

### A5 — The Transactional Developer
**Strength Dimensions:** Adaptive Coaching Style (moderate)  

**Core Pattern:** Applies coaching techniques but views development as discretionary rather than core responsibility. May coach effectively within own team but not across bilateral boundaries.

**Strengths:**
- Has some coaching technique and capability
- May be effective with their own direct reports
- Can adapt style when motivated to do so

**Growth Areas:**
- Development stops at organisational boundaries — does not invest in developing counterparts
- Views cross-boundary development as discretionary, not core responsibility
- Limited bilateral impact on talent development
- Risk of talent development gaps in partnership contexts

**Development Focus:** Build developmental orientation for cross-boundary relationships — reframe bilateral development as strategic investment, not favour; build trust-building capability across boundaries; develop accountability methods that work without formal authority.

**Organisational Impact:** Limited bilateral impact. Development investment stops at organisational boundaries. Risk of talent development gaps in partnership contexts.

## 4. Interpretation Framework

### Composite Score Interpretation

| Band | Score Range | Interpretation |
|------|-------------|----------------|
| Mature Coaching Leader | 80–100 | Demonstrates mature bilateral coaching capability across all dimensions. Can develop leadership talent effectively in cross-boundary contexts. |
| Developing Coaching Leader | 60–79 | Solid coaching foundation with specific dimensions requiring investment. Effective in familiar contexts but may struggle under bilateral constraints. |
| Emerging Coaching Awareness | 40–59 | Foundational coaching capability is developing but not yet reliable for bilateral leadership development. Significant risk of talent development gaps. |
| Coaching Capability Gap | 0–39 | Significant gaps across coaching dimensions. Bilateral talent development is at serious risk without targeted intervention. |

### Dimension-Level Interpretation

- **Strong (16–20):** Mature bilateral coaching capability
- **Developing (10–15.9):** Emerging coaching capability with development opportunities
- **Gap (0–9.9):** Significant coaching capability gap
