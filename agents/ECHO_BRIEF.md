## NON-NEGOTIABLE: INTERNAL vs EXTERNAL LANGUAGE RULE

Read this twice. This is the single most common failure point.

**Internal language** is how we plan and talk among ourselves. It describes our operating model.
**External language** is what clients and prospects see. It describes outcomes and value.

### WORDS THAT NEVER LEAVE THE BUILDING

The following are INTERNAL planning terms. They must NEVER appear in client-facing output — websites, decks, emails, social posts, webinar slides, proposals, one-pagers, or anything published:

- "flywheel"
- "3-layer model" / "Layer 1" / "Layer 2" / "Layer 3" / any layer numbering
- "maturity stack" / "maturity stage" / "Stage 0" / "Stage 1" / "Stage 2" / "Stage 3"
- "signals" / "signal matching" / "signal map" (in client-facing context)
- "entry point" / "funnel" (as positioning language for how clients engage with us)
- "taxonomy rule" (this is our internal process, never cited externally)
- "endorsed brand" / "parentage" / "brand architecture" language
- "anti-positioning" (internal strategy term)

### WHAT TO USE INSTEAD

Describe the outcome or the programme directly:

- Instead of "Layer 1" → "Research & Intelligence" or "the research we publish"
- Instead of "Layer 2" → "Leadership Programmes" or "the programmes we run"
- Instead of "Layer 3" → "Executive Search & Leadership Advisory"
- Instead of "maturity stack" / "stages" → describe where someone is in their understanding or what they need next
- Instead of "signals" → "what we're seeing" / "market indicators" / "trends"
- Instead of "entry point" → "start here" / "first step" / name the specific offer

### THE TEST

If a prospect read your copy and asked "what's the flywheel?" — you failed.
If a client needs to understand our internal operating model to understand the value — you failed.

Briefings, trackers, and internal discussions can use internal language freely. Everything that leaves the building cannot.
