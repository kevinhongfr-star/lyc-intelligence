## Strategic Problem

Look at most recruiting technology — the pitch is almost always the same: *find more candidates, faster*. LinkedIn Recruiter leads with scale. AI sourcing tools lead with speed. ATS platforms lead with workflow efficiency. The category conversation is dominated by volume and velocity.

What we rarely see in these tools is a serious answer to the question that matters most to the people paying the bill: **will this person actually succeed in the role?**

The data on executive hiring is well-documented. A 2022 study of 2,000 C-suite hires found a 73% failure rate within 18 months — and the leading cause wasn't lack of technical skill or resume fit. It was misjudgment of how the person would lead, decide, and fit into the culture of the organization.

The cost of that misjudgment isn't the recruiter's fee or the software subscription. It's the 18 months of lost momentum, the team disruption, the customers who see the turnover and wonder what's happening. A bad executive hire is expensive in a way that no sourcing-tool feature on the market addresses.

DEX AI was built to work on this side of the problem. It doesn't replace the search — it adds a layer of assessment, diagnosis, and intelligence about people and teams that search alone doesn't provide. It combines the breadth of a search platform with the depth of an executive evaluation system.

Right now, most people who hear about DEX AI file it under "recruiting software." That's a category problem, not a product problem. The positioning work in this phase is about moving DEX AI out of the recruiting-tools bucket and into the conversation where it actually belongs: intelligence about leadership, talent, and teams.

**That's the strategic problem we work on in this phase.**

## Six Locked Brand Decisions

### 1. Category
**What do people compare us to?**

**Proposed: Executive Intelligence Platform**

Not an AI recruiting tool. Not a candidate search platform. Not an ATS.

**Executive Intelligence Platform.**

This reframes the comparison entirely:
- Instead of "LinkedIn Recruiter vs. us" (we lose — they have 900M users), it becomes "your current recruiting tools vs. intelligence about your most critical asset"
- The comparison shifts from features (how many candidates, how fast) to outcomes (how good are your hires, how well do you know your leadership bench)
- It positions DEX AI in a category of ~1-2 real competitors, not 50+ recruiting tools

**Why this works:**
- It's true — TRIDENT, CANVAS, SHIFT, GRID, LENS are all intelligence products, not sourcing tools
- It's defensible — nobody else has the assessment depth + search breadth combination
- It's premium — "intelligence platform" commands higher prices than "recruiting SaaS"
- It's expandable — the category naturally includes team assessments, market intelligence, and AI agents (the full 5-year vision)

### 2. Brand Architecture
**How does this relate to the parent brand?**

**Proposed: Endorsed sub-brand — "DEX AI by LYC Partners"**

| Architecture Model | Fit? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Standalone brand | ❌ | Too much equity in LYC Partners to walk away from |
| House of brands | ❌ | DEX AI is too tied to LYC's methodology and IP |
| Endorsed sub-brand | ✅ | DEX AI has its own identity, but LYC Partners credibility backs it |
| Descriptive sub-product | ❌ | Too limiting — DEX AI is bigger than a "feature" of LYC |

**How it shows up:**
- Primary brand name: **DEX AI**
- Tagline/byline: *by LYC Partners* or *Powered by LYC Partners intelligence*
- Logo lockup: DEX AI wordmark primary, small LYC Partners mark as endorsement
- Client-facing: "DEX AI — built on 15 years of LYC Partners executive search data"

**Why endorsed, not integrated:**
- DEX AI needs to feel like a tech product, not a consulting deliverable
- It will (eventually) serve users who aren't LYC search clients
- But the LYC name is the single biggest trust asset we have — we can't hide it

### 4. Voice
**How do we sound?**

**Proposed: Measured, evidence-led — the experienced practitioner voice**

Not the AI-content voice. Not the consultant-deck voice. The voice of someone who has sat across the table from enough CEOs to know what actually moves the needle.

**Core principle:** We lead with stories, data, and anecdotes — never with pronouncement. We offer a perspective, not the truth.

| Dimension | Setting | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Opening move** | Evidence-first | "In 15 years of executive search, we've seen…" / "The data from 12,000+ assessments suggests…" | "Here's what matters." / "This is where it breaks." / Any generic-AI framing |
| **Tone** | Calm, reflective | Speak like someone sharing what they've observed | Speak like someone announcing findings |
| **Confidence** | Provisional, earned | "From where we sit…" / "Our reading of the data is…" / "In our experience…" | Absolute claims: "The truth is…" / "Everyone knows…" / "Nobody wins in…" |
| **Pace** | Measured — one thought per sentence | Short sentences. Concrete examples. | Run-ons. Exclamation points. |
| **Stance** | Peer-to-peer | We assume the reader has their own view. We're offering another angle. | Top-down pronouncements. Talking *at* the reader. |
| **Mechanism** | Show, don't state the category | "A client recently thought their top candidate was a shoo-in. The assessment said otherwise. Six months later…" | "We're not a recruiting tool, we're an intelligence platform." |
| **Humor** | Dry, rare, never forced | The occasional sharp observation. | Memes. Emojis in serious contexts. |
| **Jargon** | Minimal, precise | Real business language. | Tech bro vocabulary. "Revolutionary." "Game-changing." |

**Banned phrases — if it sounds like every LinkedIn AI post, cut it:**
- "Here's what matters" / "Here's the thing"
- "This is where it breaks"
- "Nobody wins"
- "The truth is"
- "Let that sink in"
- "Game-changing" / "Revolutionary"
- Any sentence that starts with a bold claim and ends with a colon

**What we do instead:**
- Ground claims in specific data points (the 73% failure rate, the 12,000 assessments, a named client situation)
- Frame positions as *our reading* of a situation, not the only reading
- Use narrative openings — a scenario, a statistic, a counterintuitive finding — before stating the position
- Favor concrete nouns over abstract claims
- Never say "everyone" — say "most," "many," "the companies we work with"

### 6. Taxonomy Rule
**How do we talk about our internal frameworks?**

**Rule: Outcomes lead. Methods follow. Taxonomy recedes.**

This is the core principle from the methodology, and it applies directly to how we talk about DEX AI's intellectual property.

**What this means in practice:**

| Don't Say (Taxonomy First) | Say Instead (Outcome First) |
|---|---|
| "TRIDENT scoring" | "Executive leadership assessment" |
| "CANVAS profiles" | "Full candidate intelligence profile" |
| "SHIFT framework" | "Leadership style diagnosis" |
| "GRID market intelligence" | "Competitive talent landscape" |
| "Multi-agent coaching" | "AI-powered executive coaching" |

**Internal framework names (TRIDENT, CANVAS, SHIFT, GRID, LENS, etc.) are fine print.** They appear:
- In tooltips and documentation
- In deep-dive reports (as methodology notes)
- In sales conversations *after* the client already buys the outcome

They NEVER appear:
- In headlines
- In taglines
- In pricing page headers
- In first-touch marketing content

**Why:** Nobody buys a framework. They buy what the framework *tells them.*

## The Competitive Frame

**Who are we positioning against?**

Three categories of comparison, each requiring a different angle on what DEX AI offers. We don't attack — we offer a different frame of reference.

### 1. AI Recruiting Tools (hireEZ, Gem, Dover, etc.)
**Their frame:** Find more candidates, faster, with AI.

**Our angle:** Speed is a valid problem to solve, but it's not the expensive problem. The cost of a wrong hire at the executive level dwarfs the cost of a longer search. We work on the judgment side, not the volume side.

**How we say it — by example, not by attack:**
"A company we worked with recently ran a six-month search and presented three strong candidates to the board. All three looked good on paper. The assessment flagged that one of them — the unanimous first choice — had a track record of struggling in decentralized organizations. The company had just restructured to a matrix model. They went with the second choice. Eighteen months in, that hire is still performing. The first candidate left their next role in under a year."

### 2. LinkedIn Recruiter
**Their frame:** Biggest candidate pool. Industry standard.

**Our angle:** LinkedIn is the starting point for almost every search. It tells you who's visible, who's active, and where people have worked. It doesn't tell you much about how they think, how they lead, or how they'll perform in a specific role at a specific company. We see LinkedIn as a source — not a conclusion.

**How we say it:**
"LinkedIn tells you who's in the market. We're trying to tell you who's likely to succeed. Different questions, different tools."

### 3. Traditional Executive Search Firms
**Their frame:** We know the market. We have relationships.

**Our angle:** Relationships and market knowledge are essential. They get you to the right people. But the final call — whether this person will work in this role at this company — has always been a human judgment call informed by limited data. DEX AI adds a structured, data-backed layer of assessment to that judgment. It doesn't replace the search partner. It gives them better information.

**How we say it:**
"The best search partners we know combine deep relationships with a real read of people. What DEX AI adds is a consistent, data-rich assessment layer — the kind you can't get from a few conversations and a resume. It makes the human judgment sharper."
