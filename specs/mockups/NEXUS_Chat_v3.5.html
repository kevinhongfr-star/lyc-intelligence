Readout · Strategic alignment

Your leadership team is aligned on vision, misaligned on strategy — and it's starting to cost you people.

This is a specific pattern. Vision alignment is high — everyone knows where you're going. Strategy alignment is low — nobody agrees on how to get there. The gap between the two is the single biggest predictor of avoidable attrition at the 30-80 person stage.

Why this is happening now

Up to 30 people, your direct oversight compensates for strategy drift. You sit in every key meeting, you catch misalignment early, you course-correct. At 40, you're no longer in every room. The misalignment that was always there — between COO and CFO on capital allocation, between teams on priorities — now has room to grow.

The director exits are the leading indicator. Good people don't leave bad companies — they leave situations where they can't see a coherent plan. Two in one quarter means the pattern has reached the next layer.

What to do about it

You don't fix this with more meetings. You fix it by forcing a single strategic framework that every function has to map to. Three moves:

  • One-page strategy. Not a deck — a single page with 3-5 priorities ranked, each with a named owner and a clear kill criterion. If it doesn't fit on one page, you haven't decided yet.
  • Capital allocation decision grid. The COO/CFO tension is the symptom. The cause is no shared framework for how capital gets deployed. Build one together — categories, thresholds, escalation rules. Then both of them operate inside it.
  • 90-day alignment checkpoint. Not another offsite. A 90-minute monthly review against the one-page strategy. No presentations — just: are we on track, what changed, what do we need to decide.
